    Reading the shoreline

    • The water line is the tide. It sits at the predicted height on the tide pole. Zero on the pole is MLLW, the average of the lower low tides, so it is not water depth at the beach.
    • Flags mark the next high and low. They stand where that water level lands on the sand. The beach slope is drawn, not surveyed, so read them as a direction and not a distance.
    • Waves and specks show the flow. They drift toward the beach while the tide rises, away while it falls, and nearly stop near the turn. This is a visual cue only. NOAA supplies no current speeds here.
    • The sky is the real sun. Sunrise, sunset and civil twilight come from Sunrise-Sunset.org. The moon's place in the sky is illustrative.

    Sources

    Tides: NOAA Tides & Currents. The Jekyll Marina station is in Jekyll Creek on the island's west side. NOAA publishes only its high and low predictions there, so the curve between them is a smooth estimate. St. Simons Island, the nearby reference station, has NOAA predictions every six minutes. These are predicted water levels. Wind and weather change what you see.

    Sunlight: Sunrise-Sunset.org at 31.07 N, 81.41 W. Every date and time uses America/New_York, including daylight saving time. Schedules are kept while the page is open, and between visits when the browser allows storage. NOAA API documentation.
